Community-Based Medical Sharing

What Is a Health Share?

A health care sharing ministry or medical cost-sharing community is a membership arrangement in which members contribute monthly amounts that may be used to share other members' eligible medical expenses.

A health share is not health insurance. It is not subject to all of the rules and consumer protections that apply to ACA-compliant insurance, and payment of a medical bill is not guaranteed by an insurance contract.

Know the distinction before joining.

Monthly contributions, an initial unshared amount, sharing limits, and eligible medical needs can resemble insurance terminology—but the legal protections and obligations are different.

How Medical Sharing Works

From Membership to a Sharing Request

1

Join a Community

Apply, agree to the membership guidelines, and select a program level. Eligibility or lifestyle requirements may apply.

2

Contribute Monthly

Pay the required monthly share or membership amount. This is not an insurance premium.

3

Receive Medical Care

Ask about self-pay pricing, provider arrangements, and any notification or preauthorization requirements.

4

Submit an Eligible Need

The program reviews the expense under its guidelines. Eligible amounts may be shared after the member responsibility is met.

Balanced Comparison

Potential Pros and Cons

Features vary significantly by organization and membership level. The written guidelines control what may be shared.

Potential Advantages

✓ Lower monthly cost

Monthly membership amounts may be lower than premiums for comprehensive insurance, depending on the household and program.

✓ Enrollment flexibility

Some programs accept applications throughout the year rather than using an ACA open-enrollment window.

✓ Community focus

Members may value participating in a community organized around shared beliefs or mutual support.

✓ Provider flexibility

Some programs allow broad provider choice or self-pay negotiation, subject to program procedures.

Important Limitations

! Sharing is not guaranteed

The organization is not an insurer contractually obligated to pay every eligible bill.

! Fewer legal protections

ACA essential-benefit, guaranteed-issue, appeals, out-of-pocket maximum, and No Surprises Act protections may not apply.

! Conditions and services may be limited

Pre-existing conditions, prescriptions, preventive care, maternity, mental health, or other care may have waiting periods, caps, or exclusions.

! Member rules apply

Programs may include religious, lifestyle, tobacco, or conduct requirements and can define when a need is ineligible.

! Balance-bill exposure

Providers may treat members as self-pay patients, and the amount above the program&apos;s recognized eligible charge may remain the member&apos;s responsibility.

! No Marketplace tax credits

Premium tax credits are for eligible Marketplace insurance plans, not health-share membership contributions.

Read Before You Join

Questions to Ask

What is actually shareable?

Review definitions, exclusions, waiting periods, per-need limits, annual or lifetime limits, and rules for pre-existing conditions.

What will I pay?

Compare monthly contributions, membership fees, initial unshared amounts, visit fees, prescription costs, and possible balance bills.

How are large bills handled?

Ask about advance notification, negotiated rates, provider payment, member reimbursement, appeals, and expected processing time.

What protection would I give up?

Compare the program directly with ACA insurance, including guaranteed benefits, annual out-of-pocket limits, tax credits, and regulatory oversight.

Important Consumer Notice

Health shares are not insurance

Membership does not guarantee that another member or the organization will pay your medical bills. You remain responsible for unpaid charges. Review the complete membership guidelines and disclosures—not just the monthly amount.
