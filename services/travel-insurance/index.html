Coverage Beyond Home

What Is Travel Insurance?

Travel insurance can protect you from certain unexpected financial risks before or during a trip. Depending on the policy, it may focus on emergency medical care abroad, protect prepaid trip costs, or combine several travel benefits in one plan.

Travel medical insurance and trip protection are not the same. Travel medical coverage helps with eligible healthcare expenses while traveling, while trip protection may address covered cancellations or interruptions.

Your U.S. health plan may travel differently than you do.

Networks and benefits can change outside your service area or outside the United States. Before departure, check how your current coverage handles emergency care, claims, and medical evacuation.

Before You Buy

Questions to Ask About Every Policy

What reasons are covered?

Cancellation and interruption benefits apply only to reasons defined by the policy. “Cancel for any reason” coverage, when available, has separate rules.

Are existing conditions addressed?

Review pre-existing-condition definitions, look-back periods, exclusions, and any time-sensitive waiver requirements.

Where and when does it apply?

Confirm destination eligibility, effective and end dates, maximum trip length, geographic limits, and whether activities or sports are excluded.

How do claims work?

Ask about primary or secondary coverage, preauthorization, receipts, medical records, proof of loss, and notice deadlines.

Who May Benefit

Consider the destination, duration, and dollars at risk

International travelers, cruise passengers, students, families, extended-stay travelers, and people with significant nonrefundable trip costs may want to compare coverage.

Important

Travel insurance covers specified events—it does not cover every disruption or medical expense. Known events, government restrictions, risky activities, epidemics, weather, pregnancy, and existing conditions may be handled differently by each contract. Never assume coverage; read the full policy.
